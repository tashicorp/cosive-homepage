Introduction
This document describes the tone of voice that Cosive uses in all of its messaging. It helps marketers and social media communicators use a common voice across all of the marketing and communication channels that we use. 


Cosive Tone of Voice[a][b][c]
We use Australian English. We may selectively use US English when addressing a US audience.
Cosive’s tone is expert, pragmatic, approachable, and community-minded.
1. Expert & Authoritative
Do
* Speak to the reader directly (“you”)
* Explain the why and so what
* Speak from experience
Avoid
* Distancing or passive language
* Buzzwords and inflated claims
* Alarmist or fear-driven framing
Example
Less aligned
Cyber threat intelligence is critical in today’s rapidly evolving threat landscape, where organisations face unprecedented levels of risk.
More aligned
Cyber threat intelligence helps you understand which threats matter and why, enabling more informed security decisions.
________________
2. Pragmatic & Problem-Solving
Do
* Offer realistic starting points and next steps
* Break complexity into clear actions
* Prioritise people and process
* Acknowledge time, budget, and skill constraints
* Emphasise incremental improvement
Avoid
* Idealised “perfect-state” advice
* One-size-fits-all recommendations
* Tool-led thinking without context
* Expensive big-bang changes
Example
Less aligned
Organisations should implement a comprehensive threat intelligence platform and suite of commercial feeds to effectively address modern cyber threats.
More aligned
You can start a useful threat intelligence function with a spreadsheet, a few trusted sources, and one analyst dedicating one hour per week.
________________
3. Approachable & Supportive
Do
* Use plain, accessible language
* Encourage progress over perfection (e.g. “good enough is good enough”)
* Meet organisations where they are
Avoid
* Shaming, gatekeeping, or elitism
* Implying failure or incompetence
* Unnecessary jargon or technical language
Example
Less aligned
Many SOC teams fail to improve processes due to a critical lack of expertise.
More aligned
Many teams know what works for them, but they don’t always have the visibility to compare their operations to other SOC teams in their industry.
________________
4. Community-Minded & Collaborative
Do
* Promote intelligence sharing and collaboration
* Advocate working together to achieve more than as individuals
* Support and educate the analyst community
* Acknowledge and contribute to the open-source tools and frameworks we use and recommend
Avoid
* Competitive or “us vs them” positioning between security teams
* Overemphasising intelligence sharing risks while ignoring benefits
* Disparaging others
Example
Less aligned
We help you stay ahead of threats while your competitors fall behind.
More aligned
Threat intelligence sharing strengthens your collective defence and helps the broader security community respond more effectively to real threats.

Cosive Key Messages
These key messages reflect Cosive’s core beliefs about effective security practice. They guide what we talk about, how we position ourselves, and what we choose to comment on.
To preserve brand differentiation, we prioritise our key messages on CTI, SecOps and security maturity, and avoid generic cybersecurity commentary that doesn’t clearly reinforce our positioning.
Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI)
CTI is foundational and accessible
* Valuable programs can start small with limited time, people, and simple tools.
* Most security teams are already doing some form of CTI, even if they don’t realise it yet.
* Early CTI maturity depends more on people and processes than tooling.
Sharing strengthens the ecosystem
* While not always feasible, security teams should aim to progress from consuming intelligence to sharing it when ready.
* Sharing intelligence strengthens our collective resilience.
Analysts remain central to good CTI
* Threat intelligence platforms may deliver limited value without skilled analysts to interpret and apply the intelligence.
* Tooling should support analysts, not attempt to substitute for them.
* AI reduces manual effort and cognitive load when applied to well-defined tasks. Effective use keeps analysts in the loop for context, judgement, and accountability.
CTI goes beyond indicators - it supports decision-making at every level
* CTI extends beyond IoCs to strategic, operational, and executive intelligence that informs prioritisation, controls, risk posture, and budget decisions across your organisation.
________________


Security Operations (SecOps)
Independent perspective improves outcomes
* External assessments reveal blind spots difficult to see within day-to-day operations.
Engineering and automation are force multipliers
* Engineering enables integration, automation, and sustainable scaling.
* Automation reduces repetitive workload and frees analysts for higher-value tasks.
* Integration between security tools is essential to realise the full value of investments in both technology and people.
* Disconnected tools increase workload and reduce effectiveness across the security function.
Security Maturity and Process Improvement
* Measurement is the foundation of improvement
* Frameworks such as CTI-CMM and SIM3, supported by independent gap analyses, help teams benchmark maturity, establish baselines, and prioritise improvement.
